## Homepage history


I got online in the early 90s, at first via the uni, then via the first internet provider in the Netherlands, xs4all.
Which has a really interesting and inspiring history! See [wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XS4ALL), or the book ['De hackers die Nederland veranderden'](https://www.lannoo.com/nl-nl/economie-management/de-hackers-die-nederland-veranderden-9789463812337), a great read.

In the early years, access to internet did not just mean getting online to read what others had crafted: it also meant getting a personal space for whatever you liked to put online for the world to see, in the form of a ~homepage~. 

I wrote on my homepage quite extensively for a while, coding everything by hand, later generated by a bunch of perlscripts. It lived at www.xs4all.nl/~driek - and when the tilde+username syntax had to go, it became  driek.home.xs4all.nl. 

Alas, xs4all is no more, and now even the homepages have disappeared, or are about to. ['end of an era'](https://hcc.nl/kennis/kennis/reviews/einde-van-een-internettijdperk-kpn-stopt-met-xs4all-homepages), indeed. Years ago I saw the writing on the wall and got my own domain via Freedom Internet, the spiritual successor of xs4all. But I never migrated the content of my homepage to this new site. However, I have a copy of the files, and I'm going to re-publish some of them.

And the first one is a page I put together almost 30 years ago: a little tribute as an im memoriam to the father of my then partner. It's a little time capsule in many ways: I was thirty years younger and my writing was a little cringy (I do hope I sound less pretentious these days), the original was made to work on both Mosaic and non-graphic browsers and was full of table tags... and of course, nowadays this would be a post on social media.

[David Lennox Booth, an im memoriam](https://www.heesk.nl/2026/08/15/david-lennox-booth.html)


After putting this page on the web, via mailing lists and webrings it found its way to people who read it - and dozens of netizens contacted me with details of the images, as well as many kind words. It was a tribute to a man who was in a very gentle way happily rather oldfashioned, so it seems fitting to put it online again in the same vein: as a tribute both to DLB and to the original spirit of the internet.
